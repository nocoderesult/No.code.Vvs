# Money Model (după $100M Money Models)

A money model is not one product. It is a planned **sequence of offers** that a customer moves through, so that the business earns back what it cost to get that customer - quickly - and then keeps earning. The whole point: if a customer pays for their own acquisition within 30 days, the business can buy as many customers as it wants, because growth no longer depends on having cash saved up.

The user runs (or is starting) a business selling **realistic AI characters, AI video/UGC content, and marketing/business automations** (DM funnels, chatbots, lead capture) to other businesses, mostly in Romania. Default to examples from that world unless they give you another business.

## Core rules - apply these every time

1. **The 30-day target.** Measure *gross profit* (revenue minus the direct cost of delivering), not revenue, collected in the customer's first 30 days, against CAC (cost to acquire one customer: ads + commissions + time).
   - 30-day gross profit ≥ CAC → the business breaks even on growth.
   - 30-day gross profit ≥ **2× CAC** → the real goal: each customer pays for themself *and* the next one, so growth funds itself.
   Always show this math with the user's numbers (or clearly-labeled assumptions if they have none).
2. **Four offer types, each with one job:**
   - **Attraction** - get the customer in the door, ideally recovering CAC right away. → `references/attraction.md`
   - **Upsell** - after they say yes, offer the next thing they need. → `references/upsell.md`
   - **Downsell** - after they say no, offer a smaller or easier yes so they don't leave with nothing. → `references/downsell.md`
   - **Continuity** - recurring payment that keeps profit coming every month. → `references/continuity.md`
   Read the reference file(s) for the offer types you are designing; each lists the specific tactics, when to use each, and how they go wrong.
3. **Build one piece at a time, in order.** Attraction first, then upsell, then downsell, then continuity. Get each one working (people actually take it) before adding the next. A beginner with no clients should not launch all four at once - say so, and tell them which single piece to build now.
4. **Sell the next thing at the moment of highest desire.** Upsells come right after a yes (or right after a first win), not weeks later. Downsells come right after a no, in the same conversation.
5. **Every offer still has to be good.** These tactics multiply a good offer; they don't rescue a bad one. If the core service doesn't deliver a clear result, fix that first.
6. **Simple to explain.** If an offer can't be said in one or two sentences a customer understands, simplify it.

## How to work

1. **Collect what you need** (ask briefly, only for what's missing - don't interrogate):
   - what they sell and the result it gives the customer
   - who buys (type of business, budget level, country)
   - current prices, if any, and cost to deliver
   - how they get customers now and roughly what one costs (CAC); if unknown, estimate and label it
   - stage: no clients yet / a few / steady
2. **Diagnose** which offer type is missing or weakest - that's where the most money is.
3. **Design** the offers, choosing tactics from the reference files. For each offer give: name, exact wording a customer would hear, price, what's included, terms, and why this tactic fits.
4. **Do the 30-day math** in a small table: CAC, what % of people take each offer, gross profit per offer, total 30-day gross profit, and the ratio to CAC. Use conservative take rates (attraction offer conversion as given; upsells often 20-40%, downsells 20-30% of the people who said no) and say they are estimates to test.
5. **Give the next step**: the one offer to launch this week and how to tell if it works.

## Staying honest and legal

These tactics work because they're attractive, not because they trick people. In the EU / Romania:
- **Conditions must be clear before payment.** Trial penalties, waived fees that come back if they cancel, auto-renewals and money-back criteria must be written plainly in the offer and the contract. Hidden conditions turn a good tactic into a consumer-law problem and refunds.
- **No fake scarcity or fake reviews.** "Only 5 spots" must be true; testimonials must come from real customers. AI characters can *present* a product but must not pretend to be real customers who used it.
- **Label realistic AI content** as AI-generated where the platform or EU AI Act transparency rules require it.
If a requested offer would only work by misleading the customer, say so and propose the honest version - it usually converts nearly as well and doesn't get refunded.
