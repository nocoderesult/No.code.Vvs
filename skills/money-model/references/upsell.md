# Upsell offers (Stage II - Get more cash)

An upsell is whatever you offer next. Every solved problem reveals a new one; the upsell solves it. Profit often comes from the second, third, fourth offer, not the first (burger → fries → drink → supersize). Upsells offer **more** (quantity), **better** (quality) or **new/complementary** (different).
Upsells fail when you offer something they don't need, at the wrong time (before they feel the problem), or the wrong way (they don't believe you). The book warns these are brutally effective - use them ethically.

## 1. Classic Upsell - "You can't have X without Y"
- Offer the solution to the next problem at the exact moment they realise it. You should know their problems better than they do.
- Actually do it - a business with one offer is "barely a business".
- Offer the more profitable upsell first.
- Phrase so "no" means yes: "Nu mai vrei nimic altceva pe lângă X și Y, nu?"
- Surprise and delight: if they say yes early, still give all the bonuses you'd have used to tip them over.
- Sell more in the "hyper-buying" window right after they decide to start something.
- Use free bonuses that create a problem the upsell solves.
- The faster they get access, the more they value it; hand it over before they decide when possible.
- Bundle upsells and name the bundles by customer type/result ("Rezultate rapide", "Transformare", "Minimum").
- Integrate upsells into delivery (your plan/training recommends them).
- BAMFAM: end every meeting by booking the next, with when and why.
- Upsell as many times as there are problems you can solve.
- "Magnetic middle": price the middle option close to the size you want people to move to (to push Large: S 5 / M 8 / L 9; to push Medium: S 6 / M 7 / L 9).
- Charge 5-50% for guarantees, warranties, insurance instead of giving them free.

## 2. Menu Upsell - "You don't need that... you need this"
Four steps, in order:
1. **Unsell** what they don't need (cross it out) - builds trust.
2. **Prescribe** exactly what they need, how it fits what they bought, and how to use it - as if they already have it; written, personalised instructions sell more than vague suggestions.
3. **A/B**: ask which they prefer, not whether they want it (quantity, start date, time slot, format, person, channel...). Add a nudge ("acesta e preferatul meu").
4. **Card on file**: "Folosim cardul pe care îl avem deja?" (first purchase: "Cu ce card preferi?").
- Sold out? Take payment and deliver later with a clear date.
- Let employees teach customers to "game the system".
- Two options and you want both sold: add a third "both" option priced the same as the dearer single option.

## 3. Anchor Upsell
Steps: present the expensive premium (5-10× the main offer) → expect the gasp → rescue ("contează pentru tine [elementul premium]?") → present the main offer → ask for payment.
- The premium must be real and something you'd happily deliver; sell it genuinely, or the customer treats it as fake. Some will buy it (big profits).
- Main and premium share the **primary** features; only secondary "premium" features differ - so the main offer feels like "the same thing" for ~1/5 of the price.
- Have the cheaper option ready before the reaction. No gasp? Ask for the card.
- Losing a 1,000-budget customer by offering only 100 is worse than the reverse - always keep a premium option.

## 4. Rollover Upsell - "Let's roll it forward"
Credit some or all of a previous purchase toward the next, bigger offer.
- **Who**: old customers (6+ months, "winback" campaigns), upset customers (instead of a refund - a "do over"), other businesses' unhappy customers, current customers.
- **What**: more of the same, better, or new - something bigger.
- **How**: full or partial credit, up front or spread over time (e.g. 600 → 50/month off a 12-month plan).
- Price the next offer at least 4× the credit (max ~25% discount). Credit only what's needed to motivate.
- One-time only, decide now - urgency.
- Gift card play: sell a 200 card for 20 (max 2 per person, only for others); apply it to an offer worth at least 5× the card.
- EU note: finding other companies' unhappy customers is fine via your own content and ads - do not scrape personal data from reviews.
